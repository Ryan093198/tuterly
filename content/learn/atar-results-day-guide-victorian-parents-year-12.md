Results day in Victoria lands in mid-December, and for most families it arrives feeling both overdue and too soon. Your child has spent two years building toward a number, and now the number is coming. Knowing what that number actually means, and what it does not mean, is the most useful thing a parent can do before the morning it appears.

## What the ATAR actually is

The ATAR (Australian Tertiary Admission Rank) is not a score out of 100. It is a rank that shows where your child sits relative to their entire age group in Victoria, including students who did not complete Year 12. An ATAR of 80 means your child performed better than 80 per cent of that group. That distinction matters, because it explains why a 70 in one subject does not translate neatly to a 70 ATAR.

## How the Victorian ATAR is calculated

The Victorian Curriculum and Assessment Authority (VCAA) runs the calculation through a two-step process. First, your child's raw study scores in each VCE subject are scaled. Scaling adjusts for the difficulty of each subject and the academic ability of the students who sit it. A high raw score in a subject taken by many high-performing students will scale differently from the same raw score in a less competitive cohort.

Second, your child's best subjects are combined into an aggregate. In Victoria, the calculation uses English (or an approved English equivalent, compulsory) plus the best three other scaled study scores, with 10 per cent of any remaining subjects added on top. The final aggregate is then converted to the ATAR rank. It is a relative measure from start to finish.

## What counts as a 'good' ATAR?

This is where parents often need the most reframing. A good ATAR is one that opens the door your child wants to walk through, and that threshold is different for every pathway.

Most undergraduate courses at Victorian universities have median ATARs well below 80. A large number of nursing, education, and business degrees have guaranteed entry thresholds in the 60s and 70s. Medicine and law at Melbourne or Monash sit at the top end, but they are not the benchmark against which every student should measure themselves. If your child's ATAR gets them into their preferred course, it has done its job.

There are also strong pathways that do not involve the ATAR at all. TAFE, VET qualifications, portfolio entry, and mid-year applications all exist for a reason, and a student who reaches one of those pathways is not on a lesser road. Worth knowing before results day, not after.

## Supporting your child in the lead-up

The weeks before results can quietly become more stressful than the study period itself. The work is done, there is nothing left to control, and anxiety fills the gap. A few things that actually help.

Keep the conversation low-pressure and practical. Talk through what the range of outcomes looks like, and what happens in each case. Students who already know there is a sensible plan B tend to manage the wait better than those for whom a single number carries everything.

Resist the urge to fill silence with reassurance. "I'm sure you'll be fine" is well-meaning but lands poorly when your child knows you cannot actually know that. "Whatever happens, we'll work it out" is more honest and more steadying.

Watch for signs of serious anxiety, especially disrupted sleep, withdrawal, and avoidance. If your child is struggling, that is worth addressing directly, not waiting to see what the results bring.

## On results day itself

The ATAR and VCE results are released together in Victoria, typically on a Monday morning in mid-December via the VTAC website and the VCAA results portal. Download or screenshot everything early. VTAC and school offer portals run slowly on results morning due to traffic.

If the number is lower than expected, do not rush to a decision. VTAC change of preference opens quickly, and there is time to think before committing. Many students find a course they are genuinely happy with through adjustment offers and later rounds. VTAC's website walks through the timeline clearly.

If the number is higher than expected, remind your child that the subjects ahead still need to suit them, not just the ATAR cutoff.

## The year before results day is where the real leverage is

Supporting your child through results day is important. Supporting them through Year 12 itself is where outcomes are actually shaped. Students who understand how scaling works make smarter subject and study decisions. Students who get targeted practice in their weakest VCE subjects in Terms 2 and 3 arrive at exams far better prepared than those who ramp up in the last few weeks.

If your child is heading into Year 12, that is the time to act on their weakest subject. A tutor who knows the current VCAA study design for that subject, and who works from past exams and your child's own SAC feedback, is worth more than extra hours of general study. Ask how they will show you what has been covered and what still needs attention, so you are not guessing at progress.
