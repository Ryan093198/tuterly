At some point in Year 9 or Year 10, a letter or portal notice arrives about senior subject selection, and a lot of families discover that maths is not one subject any more. There are two, three or four senior maths options, sometimes with different names from the ones parents remember, and a Year 10 class called "10A" or "advanced" that may or may not be a requirement for some of them.

This guide explains the general picture across Australia, what 10A usually means, and what to check with your child's school. One caution up front: senior maths subjects, prerequisites and school rules differ between states and between schools, and they do change. Treat this as a map for asking good questions, not as the final word. Your child's school and your state's curriculum authority have the current details.

## Why Year 10 maths matters more than other Year 10 subjects

Most senior subjects build on Year 10, but maths is unusual in how directly it does. The more advanced senior maths courses assume students arrive fluent in Year 10 algebra: expanding and factorising quadratics, solving equations confidently, rearranging formulas, working with index laws and sketching graphs. Students who are still slow or unreliable at these skills can find the first term of advanced senior maths very hard, even if they understand the new ideas.

That is why many schools use Year 10 results, or placement in an advanced Year 10 class, as one input when recommending senior maths. It is also why the year is worth taking seriously well before the subject selection form arrives.

For an overview of what Year 10 covers topic by topic, see our [Year 10 maths and 10A guide](/years/year-10-maths).

## What is 10A maths?

"10A" comes from the Australian Curriculum, which has included optional additional content for Year 10 students who are likely to study the more advanced senior maths subjects. Schools that use the label often run a separate 10A class, or teach 10A topics within an advanced Year 10 class.

Content differs between schools, but 10A commonly includes topics such as:

- surds and fractional indices
- logarithms and exponential equations
- polynomials
- factorising harder (non-monic) quadratics
- circle geometry theorems
- the sine and cosine rules and trigonometry beyond right-angled triangles
- standard deviation

A few things to keep in mind:

- **Not every school uses the name.** Some call it "advanced", "extension", "accelerated" or something else entirely. Some fold the content into their regular Year 10 course for everyone.
- **Not every school teaches all of it.** Many teach a selection.
- **NSW has its own syllabus.** NSW schools follow the NESA Mathematics K to 10 syllabus rather than the Australian Curriculum directly, and advanced Year 10 content there is organised differently. NSW parents should ask how their school groups Stage 5 maths classes and what that means for senior options.
- **The curriculum has been revised.** The Australian Curriculum Version 9 and the Victorian Curriculum 2.0 changed how some optional content is described, and schools are at different stages of adopting the changes. Ask your school what its advanced class currently covers.

If your child is in a 10A or advanced class, our [Year 10A formula sheet](/formula-sheets/year-10a-maths) and the free 10A worksheets on [surds](/worksheets/year-10a-surds), [logarithms](/worksheets/year-10a-logarithms), [polynomials](/worksheets/year-10a-polynomials) and [non-monic quadratics](/worksheets/year-10a-non-monic-quadratics) are useful companions.

## Senior maths subjects by state

The names below are the main senior maths subjects at the time of writing, from the most foundational to the most advanced. Each state also has its own rules for which combinations are allowed and how they count towards an ATAR, and some have additional courses not listed here. Please confirm everything with the school and the relevant authority.

| State | Senior maths subjects (broadly, foundational to advanced) |
|---|---|
| Victoria (VCE) | Foundation Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods, Specialist Mathematics |
| New South Wales (HSC) | Mathematics Standard, Mathematics Advanced, Mathematics Extension 1, Mathematics Extension 2 |
| Queensland (QCE) | Essential Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods, Specialist Mathematics |
| Western Australia (WACE) | Mathematics Essential, Mathematics Applications, Mathematics Methods, Mathematics Specialist |
| South Australia (SACE) and Northern Territory (NTCET) | Essential Mathematics, General Mathematics, Mathematical Methods, Specialist Mathematics |

Tasmania and the ACT have their own senior systems and course names, so families there should check with their school or college directly.

### What the levels roughly mean

Across states, the pattern is similar even when the names differ:

- **Essential or Foundation level** focuses on practical numeracy for work and everyday life.
- **General, Standard or Applications level** focuses on statistics, financial maths, networks and modelling. It suits many students and meets maths requirements for a wide range of courses.
- **Methods or Advanced level** focuses on functions, algebra and calculus. It is a prerequisite or assumed knowledge for many science, engineering, commerce, health and technology courses.
- **Specialist or Extension level** goes further into proof, vectors, complex numbers and harder calculus. It is usually taken alongside Methods or Advanced, not instead of it.

For Victorian families weighing the two most common options, our guide to [VCE General Maths vs Methods](/learn/vce-general-maths-vs-methods) goes into more detail.

## Do you need 10A to take the advanced subjects?

The honest answer is "it depends on the school". Common arrangements include:

- The school requires 10A, or an advanced Year 10 class, for entry to Methods or Advanced.
- The school recommends it strongly but makes decisions case by case.
- The school has no 10A class and uses Year 10 results and teacher recommendations instead.
- The school sets a minimum Year 10 result for the advanced subjects.

Students who did not take 10A can and do succeed in advanced senior maths, but they usually need to fill some gaps, especially in algebra fluency, surds, indices and harder factorising. Students who did take 10A are not guaranteed an easy time either. The habits matter as much as the class label.

## Questions to ask the school

Before the subject selection deadline, it is worth getting clear answers to these:

1. Does the school run a 10A or advanced Year 10 class, and how are students placed in it?
2. What does the school require or recommend for each senior maths subject?
3. If my child is not in 10A, can they still take Methods or Advanced, and what would they need to catch up on?
4. Can students move between senior maths subjects after starting, and until when?
5. Which senior maths subjects can be combined, and which are taken together?
6. Who should we talk to about university course prerequisites: the maths faculty, the careers adviser or both?

## Matching maths to future plans

Senior maths choices matter most because of university and course prerequisites. Some courses require a particular senior maths subject. Others list it as assumed knowledge or recommended study, which is not the same as a requirement but still matters for how hard the first year feels.

Prerequisites are set by universities and admissions centres and they change. Check the current requirements for any courses your child is interested in on the official university and admissions centre websites. Victorian families can also try our [ATAR planner](/atar-planner), which maps VCE subject choices against course prerequisites for a set of courses, as a starting point for that conversation.

A useful rule of thumb: if your child is undecided and has a reasonable chance of managing the more advanced subject, keeping that option open is often easier than adding it later. But choosing a subject that does not suit them can make senior years harder and less enjoyable. The maths teacher's view on how your child copes with algebra is usually the best guide.

## How to use the rest of Year 10 well

Whatever the pathway, the same few habits make the biggest difference in Year 10:

- **Get algebra fast and reliable.** Expanding, factorising, solving and rearranging should feel routine. Work through [quadratic factorising](/worksheets/year-10-quadratic-factorising), [simultaneous equations](/worksheets/year-10-simultaneous-equations) and [rearranging formulas](/worksheets/year-10-rearranging-formulas) if any feel shaky.
- **Fix old gaps now.** Year 10 difficulties often trace back to Year 8 or Year 9 skills, such as negatives in algebra or index laws. The [Year 9 hub](/years/year-9-maths) is a good place to check.
- **Practise from mistakes, not just new work.** The questions your child gets wrong in Year 10 tests are the clearest list of what to fix before senior.
- **Start test preparation earlier.** Our parent guide on [preparing for maths topic tests](/parents/preparing-for-maths-topic-tests) sets out a simple two-week plan.

Coverage of individual 10A topics can vary, so check the topic list in the app for your child's course.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 10A harder than Year 10?

Yes, generally. It adds topics and expects faster, more confident algebra. It is designed for students heading towards the more advanced senior maths subjects.

### My child missed out on the 10A class. Is Methods off the table?

Not necessarily. Ask the school what it requires. Many students move into Methods or Advanced without 10A by strengthening their algebra and covering key 10A topics during Year 10 or the summer break.

### Does the choice of senior maths affect the ATAR?

Each state scales or adjusts subjects differently, and the effect depends on the student. Choose the subject that suits your child's ability and plans, and check how your state treats each subject on the official admissions centre or curriculum authority site.

### When is subject selection?

It varies, but many schools run it during Year 10, often in Term 2 or Term 3. Some start conversations in Year 9. Check your school's calendar.

## Your next steps

1. Find out whether your child's school runs a 10A or advanced class and how placement works.
2. Ask the school what it requires or recommends for each senior maths subject.
3. Look up current prerequisites for any courses your child is considering.
4. Use the rest of Year 10 to make algebra fast and reliable.
5. Turn every wrong test question into practice before senior starts.
