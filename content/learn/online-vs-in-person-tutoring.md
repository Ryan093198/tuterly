## The short answer

For most students, a strong online tutor beats an average in-person one, and the reverse is also true. Format matters far less than the quality of the tutor and the structure around each session. The useful question is not "online or in-person?" but "which format lets my child work with the right tutor, consistently?"

## Where online wins

**Access to specialists.** This is the big one. Online removes the travel radius, so your child can work with a high-achieving tutor in the exact subject they are sitting, whether that tutor is two suburbs away or across the state. For a family outside inner Melbourne, this is often the difference between a specialist and whoever is available locally.

**No travel time.** A one-hour lesson that costs ninety minutes of driving is hard to sustain during a busy term. Online gives that time back and makes a consistent weekly session realistic.

**Better tooling.** Because sessions run on a platform, it is easy to attach a report, share practice worksheets, and keep a progress dashboard. Good online tutoring tends to be more structured, not less.

## Where in-person wins

**Young or easily distracted learners.** Some primary-aged children focus better with an adult physically beside them. If your child cannot yet self-manage on a screen, in-person may be worth the extra logistics for now.

**Hands-on or physical subjects.** A few subjects genuinely benefit from being in the room. For most academic tutoring, maths, English, the sciences, this is not a factor.

**Household setup.** In-person removes any reliance on a stable internet connection and a quiet space. If those are hard to guarantee at home, factor it in.

## What makes online work well

If you go online, a few things make the difference between a great session and a frustrating one:

- A shared digital whiteboard so tutor and student can work through problems together in real time.
- A way to show handwritten maths working, a cheap stylus or a phone camera pointed at paper both do the job.
- A quiet space and a device with a webcam.
- A tutor who is comfortable teaching online, not just hosting a call.

## The bottom line

Choose the tutor first, then the format that lets you work with them. If the best-fit tutor for your child happens to be online, the research suggests your child will do just as well, often better, than they would with a weaker local option.
