## The honest answer most tutoring companies won't give you

Every tutoring company has a financial incentive to say "yes, your child needs a tutor." So let's be upfront: not every child does. Some are doing fine and just had a bad term. Some need a different kind of support entirely. Here's how to tell the difference.

## Signs your child genuinely needs help

There are a few patterns that suggest something more than a rough patch. Your child is putting in effort but results aren't matching — they're studying, they're trying, but the marks aren't moving. Their confidence in a subject has noticeably dropped, and they're starting to avoid it or shut down when it comes up. Their teacher has flagged a concern, not just at report time but in a specific conversation about falling behind. Or they're anxious before tests in a way that's new and escalating.

These patterns tend to compound. A child who's behind in maths in Year 5 doesn't magically catch up in Year 6 — the curriculum assumes last year's skills are solid.

## Signs they're probably fine

One bad test doesn't mean a crisis. Neither does end-of-term fatigue, or the fact that their friend seems ahead. Children develop at different rates, and comparing to the highest achiever in the class is a recipe for unnecessary anxiety.

If your child generally understands the work, can explain concepts back to you in their own words, and bounces back after a poor result — they're likely fine. A tutor at this point might help marginally, but it's not where the money makes a real difference.

## Try these first

Before committing to tutoring, exhaust the free options. Talk to the teacher — ask specifically where the gaps are and what you can do at home. Try 15 minutes of structured practice daily (not worksheets for hours). Check whether your school offers lunchtime help sessions or peer tutoring. Use free resources like Khan Academy for specific topic gaps.

Give it a term. If the gap is closing, you've saved yourself the cost. If it isn't, you've also confirmed that the problem is real.
