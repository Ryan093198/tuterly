# EduTest vs ACER: which scholarship exam does your child need to sit?

Most parents starting down the scholarship path assume there's one exam. There isn't. Melbourne's independent and Catholic schools use different testing providers, and the two big ones, EduTest and ACER, are different enough that preparing for the wrong one quietly wastes months. Before you buy a single practice book, find out which test your child is actually sitting.

## EduTest, in brief

EduTest exams typically cover reading comprehension, mathematics, written expression, and verbal and numerical reasoning. The questions tend to sit closer to what children already meet at school, so a child with solid Year 5 or 6 foundations recognises the territory. Many independent schools across Melbourne use EduTest, often running their own sitting on campus. The reasoning sections still stretch students, but the maths and reading lean on curriculum knowledge your child has been building all along.

## ACER, in brief

ACER exams cover similar ground, but the style is the difference that matters. ACER leans harder into reasoning and inference. Reading passages reward students who can read between the lines rather than locate a fact, and the maths rewards problem-solving over recall. Timing and question structure also differ. ACER is common across larger school groups and a number of Catholic schools, frequently through a centralised cooperative sitting rather than a school-run one.

## Why the difference changes your prep

Because EduTest is more curriculum-aligned, preparation looks like shoring up knowledge and getting fast and accurate under time pressure. ACER asks for something harder to cram: the habit of thinking flexibly through unfamiliar problems. That's built through exposure to varied reasoning tasks over time, not a fortnight of worksheets. Prep aimed at one test leaves real gaps for the other, which is exactly why knowing the provider first saves you from wasted effort.

This is also where strong candidates pull ahead. The reasoning skills ACER prizes, and the speed EduTest demands, both reward children who are extended beyond their year level, not just patched up where they're behind.

## How to confirm which one

Check your target school's admissions or scholarship page directly, and do it for the specific year your child will sit. Schools change providers, sitting dates, and formats from one year to the next, so last year's information and other parents' advice are both unreliable. If your child is applying to several schools, you may well be preparing for both.
