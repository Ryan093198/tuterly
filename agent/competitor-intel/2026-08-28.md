# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-08-28

## Headline observation

Apex Tuition Australia has pushed a coordinated set of four WACE (Western Australian Certificate of Education) explainer articles in a single week. This looks like a deliberate move to build authority in the WA senior secondary market - a region Tuterly's VCE-focused content doesn't address. Every other competitor was quiet.

## Per-competitor findings

### Apex Tuition Australia
Four new blog posts, all on WACE fundamentals:
- How WACE exams are structured / what to expect in the written exam
- List A vs List B WACE subjects and why they matter
- WACE School-Based Assessment: how internal marks are calculated
- WACE graduation requirements: what students actually need to pass

This is a full "WACE 101" cluster aimed at parents and students trying to make sense of the WA system. It mirrors the kind of content Tuterly runs for VCE, but for a different state. Apex now has an obvious topical anchor in WA senior secondary.

### North Shore Coaching College

Low strategic signal on their own, but worth noting that North Shore continues to publish state-segmented parent comms for QLD and WA - reinforcing that competitors are treating WA as a live market.

## Cross-competitor patterns

**WA is quietly becoming a contested content territory.** Apex has just laid down a WACE explainer foundation, and North Shore continues to publish WA-specific parent newsletters. Nobody in our tracked set is doing the same for VCE at Apex's depth this week, which means the WA push stands out as a directional bet.

**Everyone else is dormant.** Nine of twelve competitors either published nothing or couldn't be crawled. That's unusual and gives Tuterly a relatively clear week to ship anything meaningful without getting drowned out.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

1. **Own the VCE equivalents of Apex's WACE cluster.** Apex's four articles map cleanly onto content we should have anyway: VCE exam structure, VCE study score / scaling explainers, SAC ("school-based assessment") mechanics, and VCE graduation / satisfactory completion requirements. Our /vce-tutoring page is a landing page - we don't appear to have the underlying explainer content that would feed it. This is the most obvious gap to close this week.

2. **Decide on WA posture.** Tuterly's site is heavily Melbourne/VCE-anchored (/best-tutoring-melbourne, /online-tutoring-melbourne, /tutoring-prices-melbourne, /vce-tutoring). If WA is not a target market, ignore Apex's move. If it is even a medium-term option, note that Apex is building the informational moat now and it will be more expensive to catch up in six months.

3. **The "alternative to" pages are a differentiator worth extending.** We have /cluey-alternative and /tutor-doctor-alternative. None of the tracked competitors run comparison pages against us or each other. Given Cluey and Tutor Doctor were both flat this week, our comparison content isn't being countered - a good reason to add /learnmate-alternative or /kip-mcgrath-alternative while the space is uncontested.

4. **Angle we could beat Apex on: the "why it matters" layer.** Apex's WACE titles are informational ("what they are and why they matter", "what students actually need to pass"). If we do the VCE versions, we can go further by tying each explainer to concrete tutor-selection or study-plan decisions - something a marketplace can do that a tutoring chain can't. Our /atar-planner is a natural hook to link every VCE explainer into.
