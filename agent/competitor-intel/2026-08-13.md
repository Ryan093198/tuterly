# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-08-13

## Headline observation

Kip McGrath has quietly launched a **"Help A Friend Succeed"** page — almost certainly a referral programme. This is the most strategically interesting move of the week: it signals Kip is leaning into word-of-mouth acquisition among existing centre families, which is a channel a marketplace like Tuterly is well-placed to match or beat. Worth a closer look at the mechanics (discount, credit, or gift) and considering whether Tuterly should introduce or promote its own parent-referral scheme.

## Cross-competitor patterns

- **Retention and referral, not acquisition, is where the movement is.** The two real changes this week (Kip's referral page, Tutor Doctor's "regular sessions" post) are both aimed at deepening or extending existing customer relationships rather than pulling in new leads. That's a shift worth watching — competitors may be feeling acquisition-cost pressure.
- **Parent-facing STEM content continues at Tutor Doctor.** They're steadily building a library of at-home activity posts. Tuterly's `/learn` and `/worksheets` sections are the natural home for a similar angle if we want to compete on parent trust.
- **The big marketplaces are quiet.** Cluey, Learnmate, Apex and Alchemy all published nothing new. Either a genuine content lull or they're working on larger releases behind the scenes.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

1. **Referral programme response.** If Kip is now publicly marketing referrals, Tuterly should either (a) launch/re-launch a parent referral offer with a clear page under `/parents`, or (b) at minimum ensure our existing word-of-mouth mechanics are visible on-site. Nothing in our current top-level paths speaks to this.
2. **Own the "consistency vs flexibility" debate.** Tutor Doctor's new post frames regular sessions as the responsible choice. Tuterly can publish a stronger counter under `/learn` or `/parents` — "how to get the accountability of weekly tutoring without locking into a contract" — that plays to marketplace strengths.
3. **At-home STEM / experiment content is a gap.** Tutor Doctor is building this steadily and we have nothing equivalent. A small series of experiment write-ups tied to `/worksheets` would be cheap to produce and gives us parent-search surface area we currently don't hold.
4. **Author pages done properly.** EzyMaths' broken author archive is a reminder that tutor/author profile pages are a category competitors struggle with. Tuterly's `/directory` and `/tutors` structure is already ahead here — worth making sure individual tutor pages are strong, indexable, and linkable, because it's a durable edge.
