# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-11-27

## Headline

Apex Tuition shipped a full Year 4 English worksheet library this week - 25 individual worksheet pages plus a hub page - while every other competitor we track was quiet. Apex is clearly running a systematic worksheet-per-topic playbook grade by grade, and they'll almost certainly roll the same template into Years 7-10 maths next. If we want /worksheets to compete on maths topic depth, we have a narrow window before Apex plants its flag there.

## Per competitor

27 new URLs, all Year 4 English worksheets. Topics cover the full primary English curriculum: sentence structure (complex sentences, text connectives), grammar (direct/reported speech, tense, homophones), text types (imaginative/informative/persuasive, formal/informal, subjective/objective), reading comprehension (The Lyrebird, "Similar Stories Different Times"), writing craft (developing and editing a story, punctuating dialogue, framing and salience), and mechanics (joined handwriting, spelling rules and word origins, long words and lazy vowels).

## Cross-competitor patterns

1. **Dead week for everyone except Apex.** Nine competitors made zero changes. Apex is the only one actively publishing, and they're publishing aggressively.
2. **Worksheet-per-topic is Apex's moat strategy.** Each topic is a standalone indexable page, not a bundled PDF download. This is a direct play against Tuterly's `/worksheets` path.
3. **Apex is building grade-by-grade, subject-by-subject.** Year 4 English this week suggests other year/subject combinations are being rolled out on a schedule. We should monitor for Year 7-10 maths specifically.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

- **Pre-empt Apex in Years 7-10 maths topic coverage.** Apex hasn't published Year 7-10 maths worksheets this week, but their template is obvious. A focused push on individually indexed Year 7, 8, 9, 10 maths topic pages (linear equations, Pythagoras, surds, quadratics, probability, trig ratios, etc.) under `/worksheets` or `/learn` would plant a flag before they get there - and it maps naturally to the app's "snap a question, practise fresh versions" loop.
- **"Common mistakes" angle is still unclaimed.** None of the competitors this week published anything in the common-mistakes or error-analysis space. That's a differentiated blog angle that ties directly to the app's core premise (students snap questions they got wrong). Worth seeding a few Year 7-10 maths posts: "Most common mistakes in Year 9 linear equations", "Why students lose marks on Pythagoras problems", etc.
- **Topic guides with worked examples beat worksheet downloads.** Apex's worksheets appear to be static. A Tuterly topic guide that ends with "snap your own question and get fresh practice in the app" has a genuinely better user experience to argue for.
- **Year 4 English is not our fight.** Ignore the content itself - we're Years 7-10 maths. But note the pattern.
