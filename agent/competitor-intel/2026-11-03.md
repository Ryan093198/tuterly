# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-11-03

## Headline

Apex Tuition is the only competitor that moved this week, and they moved hard: 14 new blog posts in one drop, all structured as "how to improve your [subject] score" breakdowns of 2025 QCE and WACE assessment reports. It's a template play - take each state's just-released examiner reports, turn each subject into a post, and own the search traffic for next year's cohort. No Years 7-10 maths content, so no direct threat to the Tuterly app, but the template itself is worth stealing for VCE.

## Apex Tuition Australia

Fourteen new posts, all published as a batch and all following two formulas:

**QCE (Queensland), covering both external exam and internal assessment for each subject:**
- Biology, Chemistry, Physics
- English
- General Maths, Specialist Maths

**WACE (Western Australia), external exam only:**
- English
- Maths Methods

Pattern: each post is pitched as a breakdown of the official 2025 subject report or examination report. This is a smart content angle - the reports are public, dense, and most students never read them. Apex is positioning as the translator.

Notable gaps in their own run: no QCE Maths Methods external post in the sample, no WACE sciences, and nothing yet for VCE, HSC or SACE. Expect those to follow in coming weeks if the template works.

**Opportunity for Tuterly:** Apex has no Years 7-10 maths content in this batch and nothing app- or practice-tool related. But the "break down the official examiner report" format is directly transferable - VCAA publishes examination reports for every VCE maths subject each year, and no one on our competitor list is systematically turning them into student-facing guides. Worth considering a Tuterly version for VCE Methods/Specialist/General, which would complement /vce-tutoring and /atar-planner.

## Cross-competitor patterns

1. **Content production is heavily concentrated.** Apex is doing all the publishing on our competitor list right now. The large marketplaces (Learnmate, Success, Cluey) are static week on week despite having the biggest URL bases - they're apparently relying on existing inventory rather than new content.

2. **State-exam post-mortems are the live format.** Apex's whole batch is retrospectives of 2025 assessments aimed at the 2026 cohort. This is the window - November through January - when students and parents search for "how did people do in [subject] last year" and "what should I do differently." Nobody else on the list is competing for that intent right now.

3. **Still no competitor app or practice-tool activity.** For the second week running, no competitor has published anything about mobile apps, snap-a-question tools, spaced practice, or AI-generated question variants. The Tuterly app's category remains uncontested in competitor marketing.

4. **Years 7-10 maths remains a quiet corridor.** Zero new competitor content targeting the Tuterly app's exact audience. The content opportunity list from prior weeks still stands - topic guides (algebra, linear equations, Pythagoras, trigonometry basics, surds, indices), common-mistake posts, and NAPLAN/selective/scholarship test preparation for Years 7-9.

## Suggested actions

1. **Build a VCE examiner-report series** modelled on Apex's QCE template - one post per VCE maths subject, published before mocks season. Low-effort, high-intent traffic, differentiated by linking each identified weakness to a Tuterly app practice set.
2. **Start the Years 7-10 maths topic-guide backlog.** Competitors keep leaving this wide open; every week without content there is a week we're not building the moat around the app's core audience.
3. **Manual spot-check of EzyMaths and Begin Bright** - two weeks of errors means we're flying blind on them.
