# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-11-23

## Top observation

Cluey Learning has effectively re-indexed its entire site this week - 1,290 "new" URLs against a baseline of 1,702, with zero removals. Most of this is a crawl/sitemap artefact (many URLs 404 or are staging pages like `partners-staging-01-25-24-ToBeDeleted`), but buried inside are several live assets worth paying attention to: a free maths worksheets hub, a parent-facing maths quiz, a Year 7 parent guide, a "20% off tutoring for grandchildren" promotion, and refreshed NAPLAN dated landing pages. The worksheets + quiz + Year 7 guide combination overlaps directly with Tuterly's Years 7-10 maths app positioning.

## Cluey Learning

Signal pages worth noting among the newly-surfaced URLs:

- **`/free-maths-worksheets`** - a lead-magnet worksheet hub pitched at Years 2-10 ("Free Maths Worksheets For Year 2-10 Students!"). Directly competes with Tuterly's `/worksheets` and sits in the same year range as the app.
- **`/maths-quiz-for-parents`** - a 15-question "are you as smart as your child" interactive. Classic parent-engagement hook; no Tuterly equivalent.
- **`/year-7`** - a parent-facing Year 7 high school guide ("Starting high school can be a really stressful time"). Covers transition anxiety, not just academics.
- **`/naplan-2023`** and **`/naplan-2024`** - dated NAPLAN landing pages with test timetables and prep funnels. Cluey keeps NAPLAN as a recurring annual SEO play.
- **`/schools`** - an Australian schools directory. Overlaps with Tuterly's `/directory`.
- **`/grandkids`** - a 20% discount offer aimed at grandparents paying for tutoring. Unusual audience segment; worth watching whether it persists.
- **`/win-tutoring-every-week`** - a competition for a free year of tutoring, used as a list-growth mechanic.
- **`/school-holiday-activities-brisbane`** / **`/gold-coast`** - note the titles say "Sydney" and "Canberra" respectively, so these pages are mis-tagged on Cluey's end. Minor, but suggests their city-page templating is sloppy.

A lot of the other "new" URLs are 404s on staging/to-be-deleted paths and old `school-terms-dates-public-holidays-australia-2023` state pages - noise.

No pricing or positioning changes detected on the core tutoring product.

## Patterns

The only real pattern this week is Cluey's: they're layering interactive, parent-facing hooks (quiz, worksheets, competition, grandparent discount) on top of their core tutoring funnel, and keeping dated NAPLAN pages as annual SEO anchors. Everyone else is silent.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

Direct-to-app angles the Cluey content surfaces:

1. **Years 7-10 maths worksheet hub as an app funnel.** Cluey's `/free-maths-worksheets` targets exactly our year range as a lead magnet for live tutoring. Tuterly already has `/worksheets` - worth checking whether it's framed as a feeder into the app (snap a worksheet question, get fresh practice versions) rather than a static PDF library. That's a story Cluey can't tell.

2. **Year 7 transition content.** Cluey's `/year-7` guide is positioned at anxious parents of kids entering high school. A Tuterly version focused specifically on the Year 6-to-7 maths jump (topics that change, common mistakes, how the app helps bridge the gap) would hit the same intent and plug straight into the app value prop.

3. **Parent maths quiz.** Cluey's interactive quiz is a cheap engagement hook. A Tuterly equivalent - "can you still do Year 9 maths?" - with wrong answers routing to app download would be on-brand (snap the one you got wrong) and more mechanically aligned than Cluey's quiz-to-tutoring-call journey.

4. **NAPLAN dated landing pages.** Tuterly has no NAPLAN page. With NAPLAN hitting Years 7 and 9, there's a direct fit for the app. Cluey rebuilds this page annually - a competitive opportunity, not a gap we need to match pixel-for-pixel.

5. **Common-mistakes topic guides per Years 7-10 maths topic.** None of the competitors crawled this week are shipping topic-level "common mistakes" content. That's the most on-brand blog territory for an app whose core loop is literally the questions students got wrong.

No competitor app or homework-practice tool surfaced this week.
