## Cross-competitor patterns

The only pattern this week is the absence of one. Mid-November is exam/wind-down season in Australia and the sector appears to be in content freeze. Expect a burst of "2026 school year prep", "back-to-school maths", and Year 7 transition content in late December and January - that is the window to pre-empt.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

Since there is nothing new to react to, two forward-looking moves stand out:

1. **Fix the blind spots.** EzyMaths and Begin Bright both failed to crawl. EzyMaths in particular is a direct maths competitor; getting their sitemap readable (try a browser-style user agent, or fall back to crawling their blog index directly) should be a priority before next week's report.

2. **Get ahead of the summer content wave.** None of our competitors currently has fresh Years 7-10 maths material in flight. This is a free run to publish the kind of content the app naturally generates evidence for:
   - Common mistakes per topic (e.g. sign errors in linear equations, misreading index laws, ratio vs fraction confusion)
   - Year-level topic guides mapped to the Victorian curriculum for Years 7, 8, 9, 10
   - End-of-year test and NAPLAN-style practice walkthroughs
   - "What Year 7 maths actually looks like" transition pieces for parents

   None of these themes appear in competitor movement this week, and they map directly to the snap-and-practise loop of the app - meaning each blog post can funnel to a concrete app use case rather than a generic tutor-matching CTA.

3. **App/tool watch - still clear air.** No competitor shipped or promoted a homework/practice tool or app this week. Cluey remains the only one with a structured online-lesson product, and even they published nothing new. Tuterly's positioning as the only iPhone practice app in this competitive set is unchallenged for another week.

## Suggested actions before next report

- Re-attempt EzyMaths and Begin Bright crawls with adjusted fetching.
- Consider baselining Success Tutoring and Learnmate blog subsections specifically (they have the largest URL counts and will likely drive most of the next change-signal volume).
- Draft 2-3 Years 7-10 maths "common mistakes" posts now so they are live before competitors wake up in January.
