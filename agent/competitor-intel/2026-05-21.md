## Cross-competitor patterns

1. **Learning-styles content is a shared moat for the franchise players.** Both Tutor Doctor and (judging by EzyMaths' tutor-rapport posts) the established tutoring brands lean on "we adapt to your child" as the differentiator. Tuterly's site doesn't have a public-facing equivalent narrative pegged to learning styles.
2. **NAPLAN and primary-years assessment explainers are an under-defended topic for Tuterly.** Tutor Doctor has a Year 3 NAPLAN bands piece. Tuterly's top-level paths cover ATAR, VCE and scholarship test prep but nothing visibly aimed at NAPLAN or primary parents.
3. **Neurodiversity / ADHD / autism content is present on both Tutor Doctor and EzyMaths.** Tuterly has no visible top-level page in this territory.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

- **Build a NAPLAN landing page or guide.** Tutor Doctor has one piece, not a cluster. There's room to outrank a single blog post with a proper /naplan section covering Years 3, 5, 7, 9 bands and prep, mirroring the depth of /vce-tutoring.
- **A "learning styles" or "how we match tutors to your child" page** would let Tuterly take the personalisation narrative back from Tutor Doctor with a structural advantage: a marketplace actually can match on style, where a franchise can only claim to.
- **ADHD / neurodiverse learner page.** Both baseline competitors touch this; Tuterly doesn't. Could sit naturally under /parents.
- **A direct comparison page is missing.** Tuterly has /cluey-alternative and /tutor-doctor-alternative but no /kip-mcgrath-alternative or /ezymath-alternative. Kip McGrath in particular is a large franchise worth a dedicated page.
- **EzyMaths' missing page metadata is a soft signal** that they're not investing in on-page fundamentals - if Tuterly ever wants to contest maths-specific search queries, the bar is low.
