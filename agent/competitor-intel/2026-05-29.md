A genuinely quiet week. Across 12 tracked competitors, the only meaningful movement is Learnmate quietly removing two VCE subject landing pages (Accounting and Economics) - both subjects Tuterly's /vce-tutoring hub could pick up coverage on while a direct competitor steps back.

## Cross-competitor patterns

The dominant pattern this week is stasis. Eight of eleven reachable competitors made zero detectable changes. That's notable in itself: late May / early June is end-of-term in most states, and competitors are not racing to publish Term 2 wrap-ups or Term 3 prep content. There's an open window here.

The one move that did happen - Learnmate's deletions - hints that even the largest marketplace-style competitor is pruning rather than expanding VCE subject coverage.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

1. **Claim the VCE Accounting and VCE Economics ground Learnmate just vacated.** Tuterly has `/vce-tutoring` but no visible subject-level pages for these. Two dedicated pages with worked examples, exam tips, and tutor listings would meet demand that a competitor has just stopped serving. Priority opportunity.

2. **End-of-term parent content.** North Shore's newsletter cadence aside, no one is publishing meaningful Term 2 review or Term 3 prep material this week. A `/parents` piece on "what to do between Term 2 and Term 3" - especially tied to the ATAR planner - would have a clear field.

3. **Audit Success Tutoring manually.** If our crawler keeps returning 2 URLs, we have no real intel on them. Someone should spend 20 minutes browsing their site directly and noting current pricing, locations, and any positioning shifts. They may have changed significantly without us seeing it.

4. **Re-check Begin Bright next week.** Fetch failure on one week is noise; two weeks running is a signal their site has changed structure.

## What to ignore

North Shore's QLD newsletter is housekeeping, not strategy. No pricing changes, no new service lines, no positioning shifts were detected anywhere in the set. Don't over-read the quiet week - the more useful action is offensive (filling the VCE subject gap) rather than reactive.
