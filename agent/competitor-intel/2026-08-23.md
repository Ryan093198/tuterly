The dual T&Cs pages tell us this is a paid-media-backed campaign (they need legal pages for ad platforms and competition compliance), not just organic content. Chatswood is being treated as a priority location.

Opportunity for Tuterly: North Shore is building named, branded programs ("Bright Spark") under exam preparation. Our `/scholarship-test-prep` page is the closest analogue but isn't branded as a program with its own identity. Worth considering whether a named Tuterly program (particularly for scholarship / selective / VCE prep cohorts) would give us something more memorable to market against.

Also worth noting: North Shore is running a free-trial-class + prize competition combo at a single location. If Tuterly is looking for a Melbourne-suburb-level acquisition tactic, this is a tested playbook.

## Cross-competitor patterns

1. **Seasonal calendar is driving the only real activity.** North Shore is the only mover, and every new page is tied to Term 4 re-enrolment or 2026 planning. Expect Cluey, Kip McGrath and Tutor Doctor to follow with similar Term 4 / 2026 pushes over the next 2-4 weeks. Tuterly should have its own Term 4 and 2026-planning messaging ready - especially anything that helps parents make the switch decision at end-of-term.

2. **Named programs vs. generic service pages.** North Shore's "Bright Spark" branding sits under exam prep as a distinct offering. Most competitors (and Tuterly) still describe services generically ("VCE tutoring", "scholarship test prep"). A named, packaged program is easier to market, remember and refer.

3. **Location-specific landing pages tied to campaigns.** North Shore built a Chatswood-specific trial + competition funnel with legal pages. Tuterly has strong Melbourne-level pages (`/best-tutoring-melbourne`, `/online-tutoring-melbourne`, `/tutoring-prices-melbourne`) but no suburb-level equivalents. A Chatswood-style playbook for a target Melbourne suburb (e.g. Glen Waverley, Balwyn, Brighton) could be tested cheaply.

4. **Everyone else is quiet.** Nine of eleven successfully-crawled competitors made zero public changes. Either publishing schedules are genuinely paused before Term 4, or content ops across the sector are thin. Either way, there's a window for Tuterly to publish visibly and own share of voice on Term 4 / 2026 decision content before the rest of the market wakes up.
