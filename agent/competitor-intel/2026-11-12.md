# Tuterly competitor intel - 2026-11-12

## Top line

Learnmate has quietly moved up-market this week, launching an entire university-tutors vertical (11 new subject pages plus a hub). It's a direct signal that the marketplace sees Years 7-10 and VCE as increasingly commoditised and is chasing higher-value adult learners. Not a direct threat to the Tuterly app, but a reminder that the "generic tutor marketplace" positioning is drifting away from where we're playing (phone-first practice for school maths).

## Learnmate

Twelve new pages, all pointing in the same direction:

- A new university tier: `/tutors/university-tutors/` as the hub, plus subject pages for maths (calculus, linear algebra), physics, chemistry, biology, economics, accounting, psychology, law, and medicine. Positioning leans hard on tutor credentials - PhDs, former lecturers, MBBS, practising lawyers.
- Two new VCE subject pages: Economics and Accounting (online). These extend their existing VCE subject coverage rather than being a strategic shift.

Nothing new in Years 7-10 maths, and no app or practice-tool launches. The university push suggests Learnmate is leaving the school-maths practice space open rather than crowding into it.

## Success Tutoring

One new article: "Summer Holiday Learning: Why a Steady Rhythm Helps Your Child Start Term 1 Strong." Parent-facing, holiday-timed. Worth noting because we're coming into the same window - a Tuterly angle on "15 minutes of maths a day over summer, on the phone" writes itself and lines up with the app's core loop.

## North Shore Coaching College

No new pages, but a large cleanup: 239 URLs removed, almost all under `/news/` (old announcements, awards, dated posts going back to 2020-2024, including the `/news/` index itself). Looks like a blog pruning / site restructure, not a strategic move. No content opportunity here.

## Cluey, Tutor Doctor, Apex, Alchemy, Kip McGrath, Tutoring for Excellence, Math Minds

No new or removed pages this week. Cluey and Tutor Doctor in particular remain static - which continues to make our `/cluey-alternative` and `/tutor-doctor-alternative` pages worth keeping sharp, since their own messaging isn't moving.

## Cross-competitor patterns

- **Nobody is launching a practice app or homework tool.** Across 13 competitors this week, zero product moves in the "student does questions on a device" space. Tuterly's app remains unchallenged in the direct-practice category among AU tutoring brands we track.
- **Content velocity is very low.** Only Learnmate (12) and Success Tutoring (1) published anything. Most sites are static. A consistent weekly cadence of Years 7-10 maths content on Tuterly's blog would pull ahead quickly.
- **Movement is either up-market (Learnmate → university) or soft/parenting (Success → holidays).** The middle - Years 7-10 maths topic guides, common mistakes, test prep - remains under-served by competitors' new content this week.

## Opportunities for Tuterly

1. **Summer holiday angle, product-led.** Match Success Tutoring's timing with a parent-facing piece on short daily maths practice over the break, anchored in the snap-a-wrong-question loop. Push to parents list before December.
2. **Year 7-10 maths topic guides.** Nothing new from any competitor this week on linear equations, Pythagoras, algebra basics, percentages, etc. These are the exact topics the app generates fresh questions for - every topic guide can end with "snap a question you got wrong and get five more like it."
3. **"Common mistakes in Year 9 maths" style posts.** Directly aligned with the app's premise (wrong questions → fresh practice) and nobody else is publishing in this lane.
4. **VCE Methods / Specialist practice content.** Learnmate added VCE Economics and Accounting but not maths. Our `/vce-tutoring` page could be supported with topic-level practice posts that funnel toward the app for Year 10 → VCE transition students.
5. **Keep the alternative pages sharp.** With Cluey and Tutor Doctor publishing nothing, our comparison pages have a stable target. Worth a periodic refresh with current pricing and a clear "we have an app, they don't" line.
